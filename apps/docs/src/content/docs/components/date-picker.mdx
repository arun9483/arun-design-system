## When to use

- A date people may type or pick: a due date, a birthday, a booking
- A range with a start and an end, optionally with times: a trip, leave, a booking window

To pick from a calendar with no field, as part of the page, use [Calendar](/components/calendar/).

## How it works

The field is a native `<input type="date">`, so typing, the format of the user's locale, the
phone's own picker and the form are the browser's. The button beside it opens a
[Calendar](/components/calendar/) in a popover; picking a day fills the field and closes it.

| Value                | Shape                              | For example                                  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| A date               | `YYYY-MM-DD`                       | `'2026-10-16'`                               |
| With `withTime`      | `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:mm`                 | `'2026-10-16T09:30'`                         |
| A range              | `{ start, end }`, each as above    | `{ start: '2026-10-12', end: '2026-10-16' }` |
| Nothing (or not yet) | `null`, or a `null` end in a range | `{ start: '2026-10-12', end: null }`         |

These are the strings the native inputs hold, in the user's local time, with no time zone.

## With a time

<Example name="date-picker-time">
  <DatePickerTime client:load />
</Example>

`withTime` makes the field a `datetime-local` input, and adds a **Time** field and a **Done**
button under the calendar. The Time field is the browser's own time input, so in Chrome its clock
button opens the same hour, minute and AM/PM columns as the native picker.

With a time, picking a day keeps the popup open, so the time can be set next; Done, Esc or a
click outside closes it. A picked day keeps the time already set, or uses the time chosen in the
popup, or midnight. Both fields stay in step: change either, and the value follows.

## Far-off dates: a date of birth

<Example name="date-picker-birth">
  <DatePickerBirth client:load />
</Example>

Paging a month at a time is fine for next week, not for 1990. `captionLayout="dropdown"` puts
**month and year selects** above the days: native `<select>`s, so typing "1990" or a phone's
wheel jumps straight there, and the arrow keys and Page Up/Down still move the calendar, the
selects following. The years run from `min`'s to `max`'s, or else from 100 years back to 10
ahead; for a birthday, `max` is today. It works the same in a range, and on its own
[Calendar](/components/calendar/).

A wedding anniversary is the same kind of date: in the past, often decades back, and with no
time.

## ISO input

<Example name="date-picker-iso">
  <DatePickerIso client:load />
</Example>

`value` and `defaultValue` take any ISO 8601 date or date-time. One with `Z` or an offset, such
as an instant from an API, is shown in the user's time zone. Seconds are dropped.
`onValueChange` always gives the local shape in the table above.

To send an instant back, `new Date(value).toISOString()` — a local date-time parses as local
time. A date alone, `new Date('2026-10-16')`, parses as UTC midnight, so keep dates as strings.

## A range

<Example name="date-range-picker-basics">
  <DateRangePickerBasics client:load />
</Example>

`DateRangePicker` has two fields, named "Start date" and "End date", and a range calendar: the
first press starts the range, the second ends it, in either order. Name the group with
`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`.

**`maxDays` limits its length**, both ends counted: `maxDays={14}` allows the 12th to the 25th.
Once the first day is picked, days further away are disabled in the calendar, and the end
field's `max` follows the start, so the browser rejects a typed end that is too far.

The fields keep each other in step: the end can't be before the start, nor the start after the
end.

### A range with times

<Example name="date-range-picker-time">
  <DateRangePickerTime client:load />
</Example>

With `withTime`, each end has its own time, set in its field or in the popup's **Start time**
and **End time** fields under the range calendar. The popup stays open after a range is picked,
until Done. Picking keeps both times; an end with none yet gets 00:00 for the start and 23:59
for the end, so the range covers whole days.

### A limit in hours

<Example name="date-range-picker-hours">
  <DateRangePickerHours client:load />
</Example>

`maxHours`, with `withTime`, caps the time from start to end: a hall booked for at most 40
hours. Once the first day is picked, the calendar disables the days 40 hours can't reach from the
start's time — from 10:00, the third day is the last. A pick whose end time would run past the
cap is pulled back to exactly start + 40 hours, and the end field's `max` follows the start, so a
typed or edited end past it is invalid and won't submit. Moving the start, typed or in the popup, pulls an end that would
then run past the cap back to it, so the range never exceeds 40 hours. With `maxDays` too, the shorter wins.

## Tags, marks and details

The pickers take Calendar's day information too — `getDayInfo`, `tagPriority`, `tagStyles`,
`onVisibleRangeChange`, `loading` and `dayProps` — and pass it to the calendar in their popup:
holidays, festivals, birthdays and bookings marked on the days, and a details card on hover,
focus or a tap. See [Calendar](/components/calendar/#what-each-day-is-tags-marks-and-details).

## Limits

- `min` and `max` are set on the fields, so the browser validates a typed date, and the calendar
  disables the days outside them and won't page past their months.
- `isDateUnavailable` disables days in the calendar. A typed unavailable day makes the field
  invalid, with "This date is unavailable." as its message (`labels.unavailable`), so a form
  won't submit it.

## Forms

The field holds its value, as an [Input](/components/input/) does, so a form submits it under its
`name` and react-hook-form binds it with `register()`, both ways:

```tsx
const { register } = useForm({ defaultValues: { due: '2026-10-16' } });

<DatePicker aria-label="Due date" {...register('due', { required: true })} />

<DateRangePicker
  aria-label="Trip dates"
  startInputProps={register('from')}
  endInputProps={register('to')}
/>
```

`setValue` and `reset()` reach the field, and the calendar opens on whatever it holds. Inside a
[Field](/components/field/), `Field.Control render={<DatePicker />}` connects the label,
description and invalid state.

## Keyboard

| Where    | Key                     | Does                                                      |
| -------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Field    | Arrows, digits          | The browser's: moves between and changes day, month, year |
| Button   | Enter, Space            | Opens the calendar on the chosen day, or today            |
| Calendar | Arrows, Page Up/Down, … | See [Calendar](/components/calendar/#keyboard)            |
| Calendar | Enter, Space            | Picks the day, closes, and returns focus to the button    |
| Calendar | Esc                     | Closes without picking, and returns focus to the button   |

## Accessibility

- The button is named "Choose date", then the chosen date in full, such as "Choose date, Friday,
  October 16, 2026", so a screen reader hears what's set without opening it.
- The popup is a non-modal dialog named "Choose date". Focus moves into the calendar on open
  and back to the button on close.
- Label the field as any input: a `<label>`, a Field, or `aria-label`.

## Notes

- The browser draws the field, so its look varies a little by browser. Chrome's own calendar
  icon is hidden in favour of the button; in Firefox and Safari a click on the field may also
  open the browser's picker. On phones, the browser's picker is the usual way in.
- Import the `calendar` and `date-picker` stylesheets, or the full `@arun-dev/ui` CSS.
- Restyle with the `--date-picker-*` and `--calendar-*` tokens.
